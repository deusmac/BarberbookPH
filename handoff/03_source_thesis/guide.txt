P If someone stops you in the hallway and asks "What is your thesis about?", this is your answer:
P *"Local barbershops still use walk-in or chat-based reservations, so customers wait a long time, bookings overlap, and it's hard to explain the haircut you want. ## 4. Chapter 1, Section by Section
P Chapter 1 answers one question: **"What is the problem, and why is it worth studying?"** Here is what each part does.
H2 Introduction
P *What it says:* Paints the picture. Barbershops are everywhere, but they still use walk-ins and notebooks, even though almost every Filipino now has a phone and internet. It also introduces the haircut communication problem ("semi-kalbo," "the usual") and ends by naming your system.
P *Why it's there:* The panel should understand the problem even if they have never been inside a barbershop.
P *What you do:* Replace the waiting time estimate with the real number you observed or were told.
H2 Background of the Study
P *What it says:* Gives the bigger context with numbers (99.63 percent of Philippine businesses are MSMEs, based on DTI), shows that small businesses often keep records in notebooks (Patayon, 2016; Santos et al., 2024), describes your partner barbershop, and lists the five problems you found.
P *Why it's there:* This is where the problem becomes real and local. The paragraph about your partner barbershop is the most important "personal touch" in the whole paper.
P *What you do:* Fill in the shop name, address, number of barbers, hours, the owner's name, and write one or two real sentences from your interview and your own observation. **Do not skip this.** A panel can easily tell when the background has no real data.
H2 Theoretical Framework (Figure 1)
P *What it says:* Explains the four "big ideas" your study stands on (see Section 6 of this guide for plain explanations).
P *Why it's there:* Every thesis must show it is based on accepted theories, not just on the students' opinions.
H2 Conceptual Framework (Figure 2)
P *What it says:* Uses the Input-Process-Output (IPO) model. Input is what you need (knowledge, software, hardware). Process is how you build and test it (Agile, sprints, evaluation). Output is the finished system and its evaluation results. The feedback arrow means user comments go back into improving the system.
P *Why it's there:* It is the "map" of your whole project in one picture. Expect to explain this figure out loud.
H2 Statement of the Problem
P *What it says:* Your six research questions, copied exactly as you provided them. Do not change these without telling your adviser, because Chapters 3, 4, and 5 will answer them one by one.
H2 Objectives of the Study
P *What it says:* The same six questions turned into "to do" statements (determine, identify, design and develop, describe, evaluate, propose). Objective 3 lists the features one by one.
P *Why it's there:* Many advisers check that each research question has a matching objective. They match one-to-one here.
H2 Assumptions of the Study
P *What it says:* Since this is a descriptive-developmental study, there is no statistical hypothesis. Instead, it lists what the study assumes (for example, that the respondents will answer honestly).
P *What you do:* Ask your adviser if they want a null hypothesis instead. There's a highlighted note in the manuscript about this.
H2 Significance of the Study
P *What it says:* Who benefits and how: owners, barbers, customers, the local business community, the proponents, your school, and future researchers.
P *What you do:* Add a subject from your course and your school or department name.
H2 Scope and Delimitation
P *What it says:* What the system WILL do (booking, gallery, preferences, reminders, dashboard, ratings) and what it will NOT do (no online payment, no payroll or inventory, no AI hairstyle preview, one partner shop only).
P *Why it's there:* This protects you. If a panelist asks "Why can't customers pay online?", your answer is "That is in our delimitations," followed by a reason.
P *What you do:* Confirm the number of respondents and whether your adviser wants IT experts as evaluators.
H2 Definition of Terms
P *What it says:* 25 terms, in alphabetical order, each with a general meaning and how it is used in your study (this is called an "operational" definition).

## 6. The Theories in Plain Words
P You do not need to memorize the textbook definitions. You need to be able to explain them like this:

TBL
Theory|Plain explanation|How it connects to BarberBook PH
Technology Acceptance Model (Davis, 1989)|People use a new app if they believe it is useful and easy. Like GCash: people switched because it saved time and was simple.|That's why the booking has only a few steps and the screens are plain.
DeLone and McLean IS Success Model (2003)|A system is "successful" if it works well, gives correct information, people keep using it, they are satisfied, and it actually helps the business.|It's where "user satisfaction" in your evaluation comes from.

## 8. About the References
P There are **46 references**, and every one of them is a real, published source: journal articles with DOIs, conference papers, official government pages, laws, ISO standards, and well-known textbooks. None were invented. Here is how to handle them:
B **Open each one at least once.** Click the DOI link (the ones that start with https://doi.org/) or the website link. Read at least the abstract. If a panelist asks "What did Hidayat and Wibowo find?", you should be able to answer in one or two sentences.
B **Some studies are from Indonesia** and have Indonesian titles. That is normal in APA. The English translation is in square brackets right after the title.
B **"n.d." means "no date."** It is used for websites that do not show when they were published.
B **"et al." means "and others."** In APA 7th edition, use it in the text when a source has three or more authors, starting with the first time you cite it.
B **Every in-text citation has a matching entry in the reference list, and every reference is cited in the text.** If you delete a paragraph, check whether its source is still cited somewhere else. If not, remove it from the list too.
B **Add your own finds.** If you find a thesis in your school library about a booking or reservation system, add it under Local Studies. Advisers like seeing sources from their own institution.
## 9. Questions Your Panel Will Probably Ask
P Practice answering these as a group. Each member should be able to answer at least three.
P **Q: What makes your system different from other booking systems?**
P A: The haircut preference feature. Customers pick a style from a photo gallery and save details like guard number, length on top, and beard trim. Most of the systems we reviewed focused only on scheduling or queues.
P **Q: Why web-based and not a mobile app?**
P A: A website works on any phone, tablet, or computer without downloading anything, and it is cheaper and faster to build and update within one school year. It is still mobile-friendly because we use a responsive layout.
P **Q: Why no online payment?**
P A: It is in our delimitations. Most local barbershops still collect cash, adding payment would need a payment gateway with fees and extra security requirements, and the main problems we are solving are waiting, scheduling, and communication.
P **Q: What if the customer doesn't show up?**
P A: The system sends reminders, which research shows can increase attendance (Guy et al., 2012). The owner can also see no-shows in the records. [[You may add a feature idea here, like a cancellation deadline, if your group decides on one.]]
P **Q: What about customers who don't have internet?**
P A: Walk-ins are still accepted. The owner encodes them in the dashboard so the schedule stays complete.
P **Q: How will you protect customer data?**
P A: We follow the Data Privacy Act of 2012. We ask for consent, collect only what we need, use password-protected accounts, and limit access to the owner and barbers.
P **Q: Why Agile and not Waterfall?**
P A: The owner will likely request changes once he sees the first version. Agile lets us build in sprints, show each part, and improve it right away.
P **Q: Why ISO/IEC 25010?**
P A: It is an international standard for software quality, and it is widely used in Philippine developmental studies. Several of the local studies we reviewed used it too, so our results can be compared with theirs.
P **Q: What is the connection to accountancy?** [[Keep this only if your group has accountancy members.]]
P A: Small business owners often keep records in notebooks (Patayon, 2016). BarberBook PH gives the shop an organized digital record of customers and bookings per barber, which can later be connected to sales and accounting records.
