# REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES
P This chapter presents the literature and studies, both foreign and local, that the proponents reviewed in relation to the development of BarberBook PH. The related literature discusses the concepts behind waiting, appointment scheduling, online self-service, the relationship between the customer and the barber, technology acceptance, and software quality. The related studies discuss existing booking and reservation systems that were developed for barbershops, salons, and other service businesses. The chapter ends with a synthesis that explains how these works relate to the present study and what gap the study intends to fill.
## Related Literature
H2 Foreign Literature
H3 Waiting Time and the Customer Experience
P Waiting is one of the most common experiences of customers in service businesses, and it has a direct effect on how they judge the service. Maister (1985), in his well-known essay on the psychology of waiting lines, explained that the way a customer feels about a wait is often more important than the actual length of the wait. He listed several principles, including that occupied time feels shorter than unoccupied time, that anxiety makes waits seem longer, that uncertain waits are longer than known and finite waits, and that unexplained waits are longer than explained waits. He also stressed that unfair waits, such as when someone who came later is served first, feel longer than fair ones.
P Taylor (1994) tested these ideas in an actual study of delayed customers and found that delays lead to feelings of uncertainty and anger, which then lower the customer's overall evaluation of the service. The study also showed that when customers felt the service provider had control over the delay, their evaluation became even more negative.
P These works are relevant to the present study because the walk-in practice in local barbershops creates exactly the kind of waiting that Maister (1985) described as the most frustrating: uncertain, unexplained, and sometimes unfair, especially when a customer who arrived earlier sees someone else being served because of a private reservation. BarberBook PH addresses this by giving each customer a fixed time slot that is visible to both the customer and the shop, which turns an uncertain wait into a known one.
H3 Appointment Scheduling and Missed Appointments
P Appointment scheduling has been studied most extensively in health care, where clinics also need to balance the waiting time of clients with the idle time of the service provider. Cayirli and Veral (2003) reviewed decades of studies on outpatient scheduling and explained that a good appointment system must consider factors such as the length of each service, the number of walk-in clients, the chance that a client will not show up, and the punctuality of both clients and providers. Gupta and Denton (2008) added that the main challenge of appointment scheduling is deciding how many slots to open, how long each slot should be, and how to handle clients who cancel, arrive late, or do not arrive at all.
P Although these studies focused on clinics, the same issues are present in barbershops. Different haircuts take different lengths of time, some customers are walk-ins, and some customers do not show up for their reservation. This is why the proponents decided that BarberBook PH should attach an estimated duration to each hairstyle, allow the owner to set working hours and slots per barber, and still allow walk-in customers to be encoded manually in the dashboard.
P One of the most common ways to reduce missed appointments is through reminders. Guy et al. (2012), in a meta-analysis and systematic review, examined studies on the use of short message service (SMS) reminders for clinic appointments and concluded that SMS reminders significantly increased attendance compared with no reminders. Since mobile phones are widely used in the Philippines (Kemp, 2026), sending an automatic reminder before a haircut appointment is a practical and low-cost way to lessen forgotten bookings.
H3 Self-Service Technology and Online Booking
P Online booking is a type of self-service technology, which refers to technology that allows customers to produce a service by themselves without the direct help of an employee. Meuter et al. (2000) studied customer experiences with self-service technologies and found that customers were satisfied when the technology solved an immediate need, was better than the alternative, and simply worked as expected. They also found that customers became dissatisfied when the technology failed, when the process was poorly designed, or when they themselves made errors because of confusing steps.
P Parasuraman (2000) introduced the concept of technology readiness, which is a person's tendency to embrace and use new technologies. According to this concept, some people are optimistic and innovative when it comes to technology, while others feel discomfort and insecurity. This means that not all customers of a barbershop will be equally willing to book online.
P These works guided two design decisions in BarberBook PH. First, the booking steps were kept short and simple so that the system is clearly easier than sending a message and waiting for a reply. Second, walk-in customers will still be accepted, so that customers who are less ready for technology are not left behind.
H3 The Customer and Barber Relationship and Haircut Preferences
P Personal services such as haircuts depend heavily on the relationship between the service provider and the client. Price and Arnould (1999) studied hairstylists and their clients and found that many of these relationships develop into what they called "commercial friendships," where the client trusts the provider and returns regularly. They found that these relationships had a positive effect on the satisfaction and loyalty of clients.
P This finding explains why many customers prefer to be served by the same barber every time. It also shows why misunderstandings about the desired haircut can be costly for a barbershop, because one bad experience can break the trust of a regular customer. In BarberBook PH, the option to choose a preferred barber and the saved haircut preferences are meant to support this relationship. The barber does not need to ask the same questions every visit, and the customer does not need to explain his preferred cut again, which helps keep the service consistent.
H3 User Acceptance of Technology
P Davis (1989) introduced the Technology Acceptance Model, which explains that perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use are the main factors that influence whether people will accept a new system. In his studies, perceived usefulness had a stronger relationship with actual use than perceived ease of use, which means that people will even put up with some difficulty if they believe that the system is truly useful to them.
P Venkatesh et al. (2003) reviewed and combined eight earlier models of technology acceptance into the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT). The model identified four main factors that affect the intention and behavior of users: performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions. Rogers (2003), in his theory of the diffusion of innovations, also explained that a new idea or technology spreads faster when people see its relative advantage, when it is compatible with their existing habits, when it is not too complex, when they can try it first, and when they can see its results.
P These theories suggest that BarberBook PH should clearly show its advantage over the current practice, should fit the habits of Filipino customers who already use their phones for other services, and should be simple enough to use even without training. The endorsement of the barbershop owner and barbers can also act as social influence that encourages customers to try the system.
H3 Software Quality and System Evaluation
P The quality of a software product can be described and measured using a standard model. The International Organization for Standardization (ISO, 2011) defined in ISO/IEC 25010 a product quality model with eight characteristics: functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability. It also defined a quality in use model with five characteristics: effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction, freedom from risk, and context coverage. In the 2023 revision of the product quality model, usability was renamed interaction capability, portability was renamed flexibility, and safety was added as a ninth characteristic (ISO, 2023).
P DeLone and McLean (2003) also explained that the success of an information system should be measured not only by its technical quality but also by the quality of its information and service, how much it is used, the satisfaction of its users, and the benefits it brings. Nielsen (1993) added that usability is not a single property but is made up of learnability, efficiency, memorability, few errors, and satisfaction, and that it should be tested with real users.
P These works support the use of ISO/IEC 25010 as the basis of the evaluation instrument for BarberBook PH. The five criteria in the study, namely functionality, usability, efficiency, reliability, and user satisfaction, were taken from the characteristics of the ISO/IEC 25010 model and are also consistent with the dimensions of information system success described by DeLone and McLean (2003).
H3 Agile Software Development
P The Manifesto for Agile Software Development (Beck et al., 2001) values individuals and interactions, working software, customer collaboration, and responding to change. Schwaber and Sutherland (2020) described Scrum, one of the most popular Agile frameworks, as a way of delivering value through short, fixed-length work periods called sprints, where a usable part of the product is completed and reviewed at the end of each sprint. Sommerville (2016) and Pressman and Maxim (2020) both explained that Agile methods are suited to small teams and to projects where the requirements of the users may change while the system is being built.
P These characteristics fit the present study. The proponents are a small team working closely with one partner barbershop, and the owner is expected to have new requests once he sees the first working version. Using Agile will allow the proponents to show the system to the owner after every sprint and improve it right away.
H2 Local Literature
H3 MSMEs and Small Service Businesses in the Philippines
P The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI, n.d.) reported that in 2023, 99.63 percent of the 1,246,373 business establishments in the Philippines were MSMEs, and 90.43 percent were micro enterprises. MSMEs generated 6,351,466 jobs, or 66.97 percent of the total employment in the country. The sector of other service activities, which includes personal services, was among the top five sectors with 87,197 MSME establishments.
P These figures show that small businesses such as barbershops are a major source of livelihood and employment for Filipinos. However, because most of them are micro enterprises, they usually have limited capital for technology. A system like BarberBook PH, which is built using free and open-source tools, can give these businesses access to online booking without the cost of commercial software.
H3 Internet and Mobile Use of Filipinos
P Filipinos are known to be heavy users of the internet and social media. The Digital 2026 report for the Philippines stated that there were around 98 million internet users in the country, representing 83.8 percent of the population, around 95.8 million social media user identities, and around 137 million cellular mobile connections (Kemp, 2026). The number of mobile connections is higher than the population because many Filipinos use more than one SIM card.
P The Philippine Development Plan 2023-2028 also puts importance on digital transformation, including helping MSMEs use digital technologies so that they can become more productive and competitive (National Economic and Development Authority [NEDA], 2023). P These trends support the idea that most customers of local barbershops already have a phone and internet access and are used to doing transactions online. This makes a mobile-friendly, web-based booking system a practical solution for the local setting.
H3 Laws Related to Online Systems and Customer Data
P Two Philippine laws are relevant to the development of BarberBook PH. The first is Republic Act No. 8792, or the Electronic Commerce Act of 2000, which gives legal recognition to electronic documents, electronic data messages, and electronic transactions (Republic Act No. 8792, 2000). This means that an online booking and its electronic confirmation are recognized in the same way as their paper counterparts.
P The second is Republic Act No. 10173, or the Data Privacy Act of 2012, which protects the personal information of individuals that is collected and processed by the government and private businesses (Republic Act No. 10173, 2012). Since BarberBook PH will collect the names, contact numbers, email addresses, and preferences of customers, the proponents must make sure that the system asks for the consent of the users, collects only the information that is needed, and keeps the data secure through password protection and limited access.
## Related Studies
H2 Foreign Studies
H3 Barbershop Booking and Reservation Systems
P Several studies, especially in Indonesia and Malaysia, have developed booking and reservation systems specifically for barbershops. Maryani et al. (2019) developed a prototype of an online reservation system for the barbershop and salon industry. Their study is one of the earlier works that recognized the need of barbershops and salons for a reservation process that customers can do online instead of in person.
P Setiawan et al. (2020) developed a web-based application for barbershop booking and transactions in Indonesia. Their work showed that booking and the recording of transactions can be combined in one system, which is useful for owners who want a record of both appointments and sales. Firmansyah et al. (2024) also developed a web-based online barbershop booking application using the CodeIgniter framework, while Ramdhani and Fadhilah (2024) developed Cutman, a web-based barbershop booking service aimed at improving the effectiveness of queuing in barbershops.
P Alamsyah et al. (2022) developed "Get Haircut," an Android application built using the React Native framework and PHP for barbershops and salons in Cirebon, Indonesia. The application allows customers to find salon locations, get a queue number, see an estimated waiting time, and view service information. The authors used the Rational Unified Process and designed the system using UML diagrams. They concluded that the application can help service providers with digital marketing and can make it more convenient for customers to find and book salon services.
P Hidayat and Wibowo (2023) pointed out that many barbershops rely on WhatsApp for reservations, which creates problems in managing queues and causes schedules to clash. To solve this, they developed an Android-based reservation application supported by a REST API, which allows customers to book services directly and helps the barbershop handle queues without worrying about overlapping schedules.
P In Malaysia, Krishnan and Mohd. Yasin (2024) developed a barber booking mobile application that allows customers to book time slots in advance, view prices, and pay through online banking. The system was developed using the Scrum methodology on the Android platform and was designed for both customers and barbers. Rusdi and Isnin (2024) developed a centralized barbershop management system with self-service queuing and booking features, staff management, and business analytics for multiple branches. They used the Waterfall model with PHP, Laravel, and MySQL. User acceptance testing showed that customers were able to book appointments and register as walk-ins, although some weaknesses were found in the cancellation feature and in the reports for managers.
P Almaarij et al. (2025) designed a reservation and customer management information system for Polka Barbershop using the Laravel framework, and Swara and Sari (2026) developed a web-based reservation and customer management system for TREVOIL Barbershop in Yogyakarta. The latter used the System Development Life Cycle, designed the system with UML and an entity relationship diagram, built it with CodeIgniter 3, and tested it using black box testing. Their results showed that the system reduced booking errors, made reservations easier, and allowed real-time management of customer information.
P These studies show that the problems of long queues, overlapping schedules, and manual reservations are common to barbershops in different countries, and that web and mobile booking systems are an effective solution. The study of Rusdi and Isnin (2024) is especially close to BarberBook PH because it also used PHP, Laravel, and MySQL and included both booking and walk-in handling. Their finding that the cancellation feature and manager reports needed improvement reminded the proponents to give extra attention to rescheduling, cancellation, and the reports in the owner dashboard.
H3 Appointment Reminders
P Guy et al. (2012) reviewed studies on SMS reminders for health clinic appointments and found that reminders sent through text message significantly increased the attendance of clients compared with not sending reminders. Although the setting was health care, the finding supports the inclusion of automatic email or SMS reminders in BarberBook PH to reduce forgotten appointments and no-shows.
H2 Local Studies
H3 Online Appointment and Reservation Systems in the Philippines
P Carino et al. (2025) developed Budget Beauty Bliss Finder, an automated booking and appointment application for salons in Pangasinan. The study aimed to solve problems in manual record keeping and booking conflicts in salon businesses. The proponents used Rapid Application Development and a descriptive-developmental approach, and the system was evaluated by salon owners, staff, and customers in terms of usability, functionality, dependability, efficiency, maintainability, and portability using a five-point Likert scale. The system received an overall rating of 4.208, interpreted as "Excellent" and "Highly Acceptable."
P Concepcion et al. (2026) developed a smart queuing and appointment management system for client services in Bulacan State University Bustos Campus. The system used HTML, CSS, JavaScript, PHP, and MySQL and was developed following the Agile software development life cycle. It was evaluated by 50 respondents using ISO/IEC 25010:2023 and the Technology Acceptance Model, and it received a weighted mean of 4.59 under ISO/IEC 25010, interpreted as "Strongly Agree." The authors concluded that the system reduced congestion and improved client satisfaction.
P Balintag et al. (2025) developed SORMS, an online reservation management system for Steffi's Garden Resort, using Agile Scrum. The system was evaluated by 18 IT professionals and 32 end users based on ISO 25010 and the Technology Acceptance Model, with mean scores of 4.68 and 4.83, respectively. The authors reported fewer booking errors and faster processing compared with the manual method. Ramento et al. (2024) likewise developed a web-based reservation and management system for Nature Spring Resort in Cagayan, which replaced a manual, paper-based booking process. Their system was evaluated using ISO 25010 with a five-point Likert scale and received "Outstanding" ratings in all characteristics, with weighted means ranging from 4.55 to 4.77.
P In the government sector, Marasigan (2025) studied the online appointment system of the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) Regional Office IV-A. Based on the responses of 300 professionals, the system showed very high responsiveness in terms of registration, processing, billing, scheduling, and confirmation. However, issues in accessibility affected client satisfaction and employee productivity. The study concluded that a well-organized scheduling system improves operational efficiency.
P These local studies show that online appointment and reservation systems are well accepted by Filipino users when they are properly developed and evaluated. They also show that ISO/IEC 25010 and the weighted mean are commonly used in local developmental studies, which supports the evaluation method chosen for BarberBook PH. The finding of Marasigan (2025) about accessibility also reminded the proponents that the system must be easy to access on ordinary mobile phones and slower internet connections.
H3 Record Keeping of Micro and Small Businesses
P Since BarberBook PH will also serve as the digital record of appointments and customers of the barbershop, the proponents reviewed local studies on the record-keeping practices of small businesses. Patayon (2016) studied 60 indigenous entrepreneurs in Marilog District, Davao City, and found that most of them did not use standard accounting ledgers and instead used notebooks as their main tool for recording income, expenses, and payables.
P Santos et al. (2024) studied the accounting practices of MSMEs in a Philippine city and found that the owners were very aware of the statutory requirements but were still unable to comply, mainly because of a lack of accounting knowledge. To help them, the researchers developed an accounting manual that MSMEs could use to monitor their operations. Ibieza et al. (2025) studied 120 micro business owners in Jordan, Guimaras, and found a moderate positive relationship between their bookkeeping knowledge and their bookkeeping practices. They recommended workshops and mentoring to strengthen the record-keeping skills of micro business owners.
P These studies show that many micro business owners in the Philippines still rely on manual and informal records. While BarberBook PH is not an accounting system, it gives the barbershop an organized, digital record of appointments, customers, and bookings per barber. This kind of record can serve as a starting point for better business monitoring and can be connected with sales and accounting records in future versions of the system.
## Related Existing Systems
P Aside from academic studies, there are commercial booking platforms used by barbershops and salons abroad, such as Booksy (Booksy, n.d.) and Fresha (Fresha, n.d.). These platforms allow customers to book appointments online and receive reminders, and they give business owners tools for managing their calendars and clients. However, they are general platforms designed for many types of beauty and wellness businesses, they commonly charge subscription or transaction fees, and they are not specifically designed around the haircut preferences of Filipino customers. P Many local barbershops, on the other hand, use Facebook Messenger or text messaging for reservations. These are free and familiar, but they are not designed for scheduling. There is no shared calendar, no automatic checking of available slots, no reminders, and no organized record of customers and their preferences.
## Synthesis
P The reviewed literature and studies show a clear pattern. Uncertain waiting lowers the satisfaction of customers (Maister, 1985; Taylor, 1994), appointment systems help balance the time of customers and service providers (Cayirli & Veral, 2003; Gupta & Denton, 2008), and reminders help reduce missed appointments (Guy et al., 2012). Customers are more likely to accept a system that is useful and easy to use (Davis, 1989; Venkatesh et al., 2003), and in personal services such as haircuts, a good understanding between the provider and the client leads to loyalty (Price & Arnould, 1999).
P The foreign studies show that booking systems for barbershops have already been developed in Indonesia and Malaysia, mostly to solve problems in queuing and overlapping schedules (Hidayat & Wibowo, 2023; Krishnan & Mohd. Yasin, 2024; Rusdi & Isnin, 2024; Swara & Sari, 2026). The local studies show that online appointment and reservation systems have been successfully developed and evaluated using ISO/IEC 25010 in salons, universities, resorts, and government offices (Balintag et al., 2025; Carino et al., 2025; Concepcion et al., 2026; Marasigan, 2025; Ramento et al., 2024). Local studies on small businesses also show that many owners still rely on notebooks and informal records (Patayon, 2016; Santos et al., 2024).

P From this review, the proponents found that most barbershop booking systems focus on the schedule, the queue, or the payment. Based on the published descriptions of these studies, very few of them treat the haircut preference of the customer as a main feature, even though the reviewed literature shows that understanding the client is important to satisfaction and loyalty in personal services (Price & Arnould, 1999). In the local setting, the proponents did not find a published study that developed and evaluated an online appointment system made specifically for Filipino barbershops, with a hairstyle gallery, preferred barber selection, saved haircut preferences, reminders, and an owner dashboard in one system. P BarberBook PH aims to fill this gap. It builds on the strengths of the reviewed systems, such as online slot booking, automatic prevention of double booking, reminders, and customer records, and adds a feature that most of them did not highlight: a way for customers to choose and save exactly how they want their hair to be cut before they arrive at the barbershop. The system will also be evaluated using ISO/IEC 25010 so that its acceptability can be compared with other locally developed systems.
